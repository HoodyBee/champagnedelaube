## A taste for the local and the authentic

I have always been drawn to things with a story and roots. A product made close to home, by someone who knows it inside out, moves me far more than an object without a face. All around us there is a wonderful heritage: skills, landscapes, women and men who work with patience and high standards. I am convinced it deserves to be valued, told and shared.

That is the common thread running through every stage of my journey, even when, from a distance, they seem to have nothing to do with one another. Cooking, textiles, communication, champagne: each time, the same wish to showcase what is well made, and the people who make it.

## From home catering to organic cotton

I started out in home catering. I have always loved good food, and that job taught me a great deal. Cooking and serving in people's homes means stepping into their private world: adapting to their table, their guests and the occasion they are celebrating, and making sure everyone has a lovely time. That is where my taste for food and champagne pairings was born, along with my sense of hospitality. I also discovered the restaurant world from the inside, with its pace, its constraints and its generosity.

## From organic cotton to champagne

I then turned to communication, in particular for champagne producers. I saw what happens behind the scenes: work in the vineyard that never stops, too little time to make yourself known, and the specific rules that govern communication about wine.

Today, through Detectezmoi.com, I help winemakers, craftspeople and entrepreneurs with their day-to-day communication: social media, online visibility, photography. My role: making sure their know-how is seen and understood, while they stay focused on their craft.

## The turning point: the Aube deserves to be better known

Working in the world of champagne, I realised something: the Aube was not valued as it deserved. It is not that I do not love the other vineyards, quite the opposite. The whole of Champagne is a magnificent region, and each of its terroirs has its treasures. But for the Aube, there was real work to be done.

With the [Côte des Bar](/la-cote-des-bar), [Montgueux](/montgueux) and [Villenauxe-la-Grande](/villenauxe-la-grande), the Aube accounts for more than a fifth of the Champagne vineyard. Its slopes, villages and winemakers deserved to have their story told more often. Champagne de l'Aube was born from that observation.

## Winemakers out of the ordinary

Meeting after meeting, I discovered passionate winemakers, wonderful, genuine people who tend their vines and make their wines with rare dedication. Their champagnes have real personality: you can taste a terroir, a way of working, a family story. They are extraordinary products, far from the standard mould, and they are the ones I want to shine a light on.

What moves me about them is their consistency: it is often the same hands that prune the vines, harvest, make the wines and welcome you at the estate. When you taste their cuvées while listening to them talk about their plots, champagne never tastes quite the same again. That is the experience I want to share with you.
